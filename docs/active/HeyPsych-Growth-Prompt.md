A clinician should understand within ten seconds:

- Which options fit this practice.
- Which options to eliminate.
- What the realistic cost is.
- Which limitation could become expensive later.
- What evidence supports the recommendation.
- What to do next.

If the page cannot do that, fix it before adding another URL.

Prioritize opportunities using a practical estimate:

BUYING INTENT × UNRESOLVED BUYER PAIN × HEYPSYCH ADVANTAGE × PRODUCT CONVERSION POTENTIAL, divided by implementation and maintenance cost.

State assumptions. Do not invent keyword volumes, revenue projections, attribution, or numerical certainty. A well-supported directional priority beats a spreadsheet of fictional precision.

## 5. TAKE THE HIGH-INTENT QUERIES PERSONALLY

Attack the moments closest to a purchase or switch:

- “[Product] vs [Product]”: make a conditional verdict immediately.
- “[Product] alternatives”: organize around the reason for leaving.
- “[Product] pricing”: expose the difference between advertised entry price and a realistic configuration.
- “Best [category] for [practice]”: apply that practice's actual constraints.
- “[Product] limitations”: identify documented constraints and who they affect.
- “Switch from [Product]”: explain verified export, migration, overlap, and onboarding requirements.

Do not invent dissatisfaction or imply misconduct. Criticize documented limitations with exact scope, dates, and sources. Distinguish “not documented,” “requires a paid tier,” “requires an integration,” and “not supported.”

Avoid cowardly comparisons such as “both are excellent choices.” Say:

CHOOSE A IF…
CHOOSE B IF…
CHOOSE NEITHER IF…
HERE IS THE CONDITION THAT CHANGES THE ANSWER.

When evidence cannot support a winner, say which missing fact decides it and give the buyer a precise question to ask.

## 6. BUILD THE DATABASE COMPETITORS SHOULD HAVE BUILT

Use one structured source of truth. Never manually replicate a product fact across thirty pages.

Capture verified product attributes across:

- Category, professions, practice size, settings, best fit, and poor fit.
- Pricing, currency, billing interval, commitment, per-user basis, minimum seats, plans, trials, and add-ons.
- Documentation, scheduling, portal, billing, claims, telehealth, AI, eRx, EPCS, medication workflows, and outcome measurement.
- Integrations, migration, exports, support, onboarding, and documented limitations.
- Vendor security statements, BAA availability and conditions, applicable attestations, and their documented scope.
- Source URLs, verification dates, applicable plans, evidence status, and alternatives.

Store provenance at the claim level where practical. One general source link is not evidence for twenty unrelated assertions.

Represent YES, NO, UNKNOWN, and CONDITIONAL distinctly. A paid add-on is not an included feature. An integration is not native support. A vendor statement is not independent verification.

One verified update should propagate to product pages, categories, guides, comparisons, alternatives, the matcher, and Practice Architect wherever the existing implementation supports it.

Keep this maintainable. Extend the existing data model before inventing infrastructure.

## 7. PRICING INTELLIGENCE: MAKE THE REAL BILL VISIBLE

Make vague pricing pages look useless by comparison.

Where verified data permits, model realistic scenarios: a solo cash-pay therapist, an insurance-heavy solo practice, a prescriber needing EPCS, and a group practice.

Show:

BASE SUBSCRIPTION + REQUIRED ADD-ONS + KNOWN USAGE CHARGES + ONE-TIME COSTS.

Separate recurring cost from onboarding and migration. Specify seats, usage, billing cadence, and assumptions. Show first-year totals only when the inputs support them. Make annual and monthly billing differences explicit.

Label estimates as estimates. Never turn quote-based or unknown pricing into a fabricated total. Show which missing charges could change the result.

Build a calculator into existing components if it materially improves the decision. A transparent calculation is more valuable than another thousand words.

## 8. FIT LOGIC MUST HAVE TEETH

Recommendations must change when the practice changes.

Therapist criteria: documentation, recurring appointments, scheduling, portal, telehealth, payments, insurance billing, and usability evidence.

Psychiatrist criteria: prescribing, EPCS, medication workflows, PDMP and lab support where documented, prior-authorization workflow, claims, telehealth, and psychiatry documentation.

PMHNP criteria: prescribing, EPCS, medication workflows, solo operations, documented multi-state workflow support, claims, telehealth, and affordability.

Group-practice criteria: roles, permissions, supervision workflows, centralized billing, reporting, staffing, migration, and seat economics.

Separate hard requirements from preferences. A product that fails a verified nonnegotiable requirement cannot buy its way to first place with cheaper pricing.

Store persona weights and the reasons behind them. Normalize weights to the stated total. Calculate published scores from actual inputs; do not hand-author impressive numbers.

Keep evidence coverage separate from fit. Unknown data does not establish absence, safety, or suitability. Withhold numerical scores when coverage is insufficient. Explain what could change the recommendation.

Show WHY IT WON, WHY IT LOST POINTS, and WHO SHOULD SKIP IT. Disclose material commercial relationships. Compensation must not silently change rankings.

## 10. EARN INDEXATION

Every new indexed page must contain at least three concrete contributions that materially improve the buying decision. At least one must involve substantive evidence, analysis, or useful functionality beyond rearranging vendor descriptions.

Examples:

- Verified total-cost scenarios.
- Profession-specific disqualifiers and ranking logic.
- A documented limitation with practical consequences.
- Original comparison calculations.
- Verified migration or integration constraints.
- A useful interactive decision tool.
- Properly collected first-party findings with sample size and limitations.

Different headings, extra FAQs, and longer copy are not information gain.

Do not auto-index every profession × state × size × payer × feature combination. State pages require verified state-specific differences that change the recommendation.

Filter URLs such as ?subcategory=... remain browsing states unless independent intent and substantive content justify an editorial page. Choose canonical and indexing treatment based on actual equivalence and current guidance; do not blindly canonicalize materially different content or use noindex as a substitute for fixing duplication.

## 16. MEASURE THE BUYING JOURNEY

Use existing analytics and Search Console access when available. Establish the baseline before claiming improvement.

Track meaningful events where authorized: recommendation engagement, comparison use, matcher start and completion, Practice Architect use, and appropriate outbound vendor clicks.

Treat these as distinct steps. A click is not a sale. Do not invent revenue attribution.

After the initial cluster, prioritize actual signals:

- Relevant queries with high impressions and positions around 4–20.
- Weak click-through relative to intent and position.
- Buying modifiers appearing repeatedly.
- Pages that attract traffic but fail to move users toward a decision.
- Growing pages with identifiable gaps.
- Cannibalization or stale facts that reduce usefulness.

Refresh and consolidate before multiplying URLs. Report ranking changes as observations, not proof of causation. Account for indexing and measurement lag.

## 17. EXECUTION LOOP AND SHIP GATE

For each cycle:

1. Inspect live pages, repository conventions, current search results, and available demand evidence.
2. Select one coherent, high-value deliverable and state the buying problem it solves.
3. Verify volatile facts from primary sources and record provenance.
4. Implement complete data, copy, UI, metadata, and contextual links.
5. Validate calculations, important claims, rendering, indexability, and the next action.
6. Ship through the authorized release process or leave an exact, validated change ready for required approval.
7. Report the result and choose the next evidence-backed improvement.

Do not pass a page because you awarded yourself 9/10. Produce observable evidence:

- A distinct intent and an obvious answer.
- At least three substantive contributions.
- Traceable important claims and explicit unknowns.
- Correct persona logic and arithmetic.
- A useful limitation for each recommendation.
- Working acquisition and product links.
- Successful required checks and no known critical regression.

If a gate fails, fix it or withhold that page from publication. Continue independent work.

Report only what matters: what changed, affected routes, buyer benefit, validation results, release status, remaining uncertainty, and the next highest-value action. Include commit or preview references when available.

Never claim research you did not perform, tests you did not run, deployment you did not complete, or outcomes you did not measure.
