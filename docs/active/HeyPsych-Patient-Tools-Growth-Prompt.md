## 3. THE FIRST WIN IS CLARITY

Within ten seconds, a person should understand:

- Whether this page addresses their goal.
- Two or three plausible options and why they differ.
- What is actually free versus trial-only.
- The type of support each option provides.
- One meaningful limitation or reason to skip each option.
- A useful next step.

An exhausted person should not have to read an essay to find the price or discover that the advertised therapist is a chatbot.

Use plain language, readable type, accessible controls, short comparisons, and progressive disclosure. Let people explore without first surrendering an email address or sensitive history.

## 4. FIX TRUST DEBT BEFORE SCALING IT

Inspect existing ratings, review counts, evidence badges, privacy grades, HIPAA labels, availability claims, and medical-review bylines.

For each, determine its source, meaning, scope, calculation or review process, and verification date.

Do not assume an existing field is accurate because it is already live. Do not accuse a vendor or prior author of fabrication without evidence.

Replace unsupported certainty with a precise statement or unknown status. Remove unsubstantiated badges from publication until supported. Never silently manufacture a source to preserve the current design.

Distinguish:

- App-store satisfaction from evidence of clinical benefit.
- A researched technique from research on this exact app.
- A vendor privacy statement from an independent assessment.
- A consumer wellness app from a clinical service or regulated product.
- Editorial review from actual medical review.

Check stale products, discontinued offerings, changed ownership, dead store links, renamed services, and unavailable regions before recommending them.

One widely reused incorrect badge can do more damage than twelve missing articles.

## 6. TAKE THE QUESTIONS OTHER SITES EVADE

For every high-intent topic, investigate:

WHAT DOES FREE ACTUALLY INCLUDE?

List useful features available without payment, relevant caps, account requirements, and whether payment details are needed. A seven-day trial is not a permanent free plan.

WHAT IS THE REAL COST?

Show billing cadence, amount charged upfront, post-trial renewal, important restrictions, and the source and date. A monthly equivalent billed annually must be labeled as such.

WHAT AM I ACTUALLY GETTING?

Self-guided content, tracking, peer interaction, AI conversation, coaching, licensed therapy, psychiatry, and prescription digital therapeutics are different offerings. Define them clearly and verify the particular product or plan.

WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS A BAD FIT?

Required reading, notification load, inaccessible design, limited exports, paywalls around key features, unsupported languages, geography, eligibility, or a support level that does not match the user's stated preference.

WHAT HAPPENS IF I LEAVE?

Investigate documented cancellation, account deletion, data export, retention, and subscription management. Distinguish canceling a subscription from deleting an account or its data.

Do not soften a documented limitation because it weakens a vendor's sales pitch. State the scope accurately and link the evidence.

## 7. MAKE COMPARISONS DECISIVE

Start with:

CHOOSE A IF…
CHOOSE B IF…
CHOOSE NEITHER IF…

Compare dimensions relevant to that choice:

- Real free functionality.
- Total subscription commitment and renewal.
- Daily effort and interaction style.
- Content, tools, or professional access included.
- Platforms, accessibility, and languages where verified.
- Privacy details and unresolved questions.
- Evidence and its limitations.
- Export, cancellation, and portability.

Do not automatically award “best overall.” Use a conditional verdict when preferences determine the winner.

Alternatives pages should start with the reason for leaving: cost, too much content, unwanted notifications, limited tracking, privacy preferences, or a need for human care.

Do not recommend a chatbot as an equivalent replacement for licensed therapy. Do not treat every meditation app as interchangeable with structured clinical treatment.

## 8. BUILD ONE VERIFIED PATIENT-TOOLS DATA MODEL

Extend existing records with useful fields rather than hardcoding facts into articles.

IDENTITY AND ACCESS

Name, developer, specific offering, category memberships, official links, app-store links, current availability, platforms, region, languages, age eligibility, account requirements, and last checked date.

SUPPORT AND FIT

Support type, intended goals, daily effort, interaction style, human involvement, professional credentials where relevant, accessibility features, useful free functionality, limitations, and reasons to choose another option.

PRICE

Currency, region, channel, plan, amount, billing period, upfront charge, trial duration, renewal terms, free-plan restrictions, optional purchases, discounts with eligibility, and coverage claims with limitations.

PRIVACY

Policy URL and effective date, account requirement, documented collection, stated sharing purposes, advertising or tracking disclosures, export, deletion, retention, AI-data practices where stated, and unresolved questions.

EVIDENCE

Exact app or intervention studied, version where known, study population, design, comparator, sample size, outcomes, duration, sponsorship, limitations, citation, and review status.

PROVENANCE

Claim-level source, source type, verified date, applicable offering and region, reviewer where real, and status: verified, vendor-reported, conditional, unknown, outdated, or unavailable.

Keep unknown, not applicable, and no distinct. Avoid general badges that hide important differences between plans or offerings.

One corrected fact should improve every page and tool that consumes it.

## 9. EVIDENCE MUST MATCH THE CLAIM

A product using CBT language does not automatically inherit every CBT study. A promising uncontrolled study does not establish superiority. Popularity and star ratings do not establish effectiveness.

Use current primary research and authoritative clinical sources for health-related claims. Verify regulatory claims against the relevant regulator's records, including the exact product, indication, population, jurisdiction, and terminology.

Separate evidence of feasibility, satisfaction, symptom change, comparative benefit, and durability. Do not imply certainty beyond the study design or population.

Make research summaries understandable without deleting limitations. Do not fabricate clinical review; route clinical recommendations and high-risk content through the actual qualified review process. Complete implementation and keep unsupported clinical claims unpublished if that review is unavailable.

Never market an app as a guaranteed cure, a diagnosis, or a reason to start, stop, or alter prescribed treatment.

## 10. PRIVACY SHOULD BE EXPLAINED, NOT DECORATED

Replace unexplained letter grades with a transparent rubric or factual comparison.

Show what was reviewed, which offering the policy covers, when it was checked, and what remains unclear. A published policy review is not a technical security audit. Do not claim hands-on testing or tracker inspection unless it occurred.

Do not use a HIPAA checkbox as a universal consumer-app safety score. Verify whether and how a claim applies to the specific relationship and offering; explain privacy practices in terms the user can act on.

Avoid absolute statements such as “your data is never shared” unless the scope is documented and accurately described. Do not infer “no sharing” from silence.

Offer meaningful preference filters only when data supports them: usable without an account, documented export, documented deletion process, or specific stated data practices.

## 11. BUILD A SMALL USEFUL CHOOSER, IF NEEDED

First inspect existing discovery and comparison capabilities. Improve them before building a second tool.

If a patient chooser is justified, ask only questions that change the result:

- What would you like help with?
- Do you want self-guided tools, tracking, or professional support?
- What is your budget?
- Which device or access format do you need?
- Which verified preferences matter, such as account requirements or daily effort?

Return a short, explainable set of options with evidence, cost, limitations, and an editable rationale.

Call this matching preferences to tools. Do not diagnose, predict treatment response, or invent a clinical suitability percentage. Keep evidence confidence separate from preference fit.

Do not ask for trauma narratives, medication lists, or diagnostic history just to recommend an app. Avoid unrestricted symptom chat unless a separately reviewed clinical and privacy design genuinely requires it.

If an existing flow accepts distress disclosures, inspect and validate its established escalation handling. Never route an explicit immediate-danger disclosure into an affiliate funnel. Keep urgent-help resources accessible, localized through verified sources, and separate from commercial recommendations. Do not claim automated risk detection is reliable or diagnostic.

## 12. DESIGN FOR PEOPLE, NOT COMPULSIVE ENGAGEMENT

A successful visit may be short: the person finds a free tool, understands its limits, and leaves with a useful next step.

No fear-based copy, diagnostic bait, guilt streaks, distress-triggered upsells, artificial countdowns, or forced signup before essential information. Avoid suggesting that skipping an app purchase means neglecting one's health.

Make reminder preferences and exits easy. Use calm motion defaults, readable comparisons, keyboard support, labeled inputs, and mobile layouts that remain usable under cognitive load.

For minors and caregiver-oriented tools, verify age eligibility and applicable safeguards before publishing recommendations. Do not build unreviewed child profiling or sensitive-data collection into discovery flows.

Commercial relationships must be visible. Sponsorship cannot silently alter rankings. Free and noncommercial options deserve the same evidence standard and visibility as paid products.

## 13. PAGE TEMPLATE: ANSWER, EVIDENCE, ACTION

ABOVE THE FOLD

A direct answer, a few conditional recommendations, actual free or paid status, a support-type label, and a useful next action.

QUICK COMPARISON

Columns chosen for this question: useful free features, paid commitment, support type, daily effort, key limitation, and evidence or privacy detail where relevant.

WHY THESE OPTIONS

Explain eligibility, selection criteria, evidence limitations, and commercial disclosures.

APP BREAKDOWNS

Best fit. What it actually does. What is free. What costs extra. Important limitation. Privacy consideration. Evidence status. Choose it if. Consider something else if. Link to the existing product record.

HOW TO CHOOSE

Resolve the practical decision rather than repeating generic mental-health advice.

QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS

Answer real, closely related questions. Avoid unsupported personalized treatment advice.

NEXT STEP

Compare a short list, use a relevant existing discovery flow, visit the verified official destination, or explore appropriate professional-care resources. Choose the action that fits the person's goal, not the highest payout.

## 14. THREE SUBSTANTIVE CONTRIBUTIONS OR NO NEW INDEXED PAGE

Each new indexed page must materially improve the answer in at least three ways, with at least one contribution beyond rearranged vendor copy:

- Verified free-feature comparison.
- Transparent annual and renewal cost calculation.
- A privacy comparison with explicit unknowns.
- App-specific evidence analysis.
- A practical export or cancellation comparison.
- A useful interactive chooser or calculator.
- Verified accessibility or eligibility distinctions.
- Documented hands-on findings from testing that actually happened.

Extra headings, a longer introduction, and generic FAQs do not qualify.

Filters remain browsing states unless distinct intent and substantive content justify editorial pages. Do not auto-generate every condition × age × gender × state × platform × price combination.

Do not manufacture diagnostic quizzes as SEO bait. Any existing screening instrument requires valid permissions, faithful scoring, appropriate interpretation and review, privacy controls, and clear limits. A marketing quiz cannot borrow clinical authority.

## 16. TECHNICAL SEO AND ACCURATE QUOTATION

Verify meaningful indexable HTML, correct status, unique title and H1, appropriate canonical, intentional indexability, sitemap inclusion, breadcrumbs, mobile rendering, accessibility, and working links.

Check current official search-engine and structured-data guidance before implementation. Mark up only eligible content visible to users. Do not represent editorial preference scores as user ratings or republish external review counts as HeyPsych's own reviews.

Include concise answer sections that name the relevant app, goal, condition, cost when verified, limitation, and source. Keep volatile facts dated. Make editorial judgments distinguishable from vendor statements and research findings.

Make the page easy to quote accurately without hidden AI instructions or promises of AI citations. Clarity is useful to people and machines.

## 17. MEASURE USEFUL DECISIONS WITHOUT PROFILING DISTRESS

Use existing authorized analytics and Search Console. Establish a baseline before claiming gains.

Track relevant page engagement, comparison use, chooser completion, official-destination clicks, and appropriate care-navigation actions. A click is not improved mental health, treatment success, or revenue.

Prefer aggregate events and minimal data. Do not send symptoms, diagnoses, journals, screening responses, sensitive query text, or condition-revealing user-level URLs to advertising systems. Inspect analytics payloads and referrers before introducing events on sensitive flows. Use nonsensitive aggregate identifiers where appropriate and existing consent controls.

Do not introduce session replay on sensitive interactions or distress-based retargeting. Do not collect sensitive answers merely because a marketing dashboard could use them.

Learn from demand, broken links, stale facts, confusing comparisons, and aggregate task completion. Publish user findings only with appropriate permission and clear methodology; never invent testimonials or outcome data.
