Episodes are not attributable to a substance or another medical condition and are not better explained by other primary psychiatric disorders. ## Diagnostic Codes - **DSM-5**: 296.2x (single episode), 296.3x (recurrent) - **ICD-10**: F32.x (single episode), F33.x (recurrent) ## Prevalence 12-month prevalence ~7% adults; higher in females. Substantial global burden with high recurrence and disability. ## Age of Onset Peak onset late adolescence to mid-20s; can occur across the lifespan. ## Symptoms ## Treatment Options ### Psychotherapy - Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) — behavioral activation, cognitive restructuring, relapse prevention. - Interpersonal Psychotherapy (IPT) — role disputes/transitions, grief, social support. - Behavioral Activation (BA) — structured activity scheduling and reward re-engagement. - Acceptance & Commitment Therapy (ACT) or Mindfulness-Based CBT as adjuncts. - Family/couples therapy when relational stressors contribute. ### Medications - First-line antidepressants: SSRIs (sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine, paroxetine, citalopram). - SNRIs (venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine, duloxetine, levomilnacipran) — consider if pain/fatigue prominent; monitor blood pressure. - Bupropion — energizing, fewer sexual side effects; avoid in seizure/eating disorders. - Mirtazapine — helpful for insomnia, low appetite/weight loss. - Tricyclics (e.g., nortriptyline) and MAOIs — effective but second/third-line due to side-effect and interaction profiles. - Augmentation for partial response: aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, quetiapine XR; lithium (suicide risk reduction); liothyronine (T3) in selected cases; buspirone or bupropion combo; psychostimulants/modafinil for residual fatigue (specialist-guided). - Peripartum: weigh maternal-fetal risks/benefits; sertraline often favored; coordinate with obstetrics. - Geriatrics: start low/go slow; monitor hyponatremia; consider QTc with citalopram; watch drug interactions. ## Prognosis Course ranges from single episode to recurrent/chronic. Early treatment, full remission, and maintenance reduce relapse/recurrence and suicide risk. Comorbidity (anxiety, substance use, medical illness) and psychosocial stress worsen outcomes. --- *Source: HeyPsych.com - Evidence-based mental health information* *URL: https://www.heypsych.com/conditions/major-depressive-disorder*"
